# Synthesized Literature Matrix

This matrix synthesizes core empirical and conceptual studies underpinning the investigation of crowdsourced academic advice, source trust, and student decision-making.

| Citation (APA 7th) | Theoretical Framework | Context & Methodology | Key Findings / Insights | Direct Relevance to Current Study |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mayer, Davis, & Schoorman (1995)** | Integrative Model of Organizational Trust | Conceptual synthesis; organizational behavior | Trust is multidimensional, determined by perceived **Ability**, **Benevolence**, and **Integrity**, moderated by perceived risk and propensity to trust. | Provides the core psychometric dimensions to measure and compare trust across seniors, friends, and anonymous contributors. |
| **Sussman & Siegal (2003)** | Information Adoption Model (IAM); Dual-process theory | Quantitative survey ($N=121$); knowledge adoption in organizations | Information adoption is driven by **Argument Quality** (central route) and **Source Credibility** (peripheral route). | Informs the operationalization of perceived information quality and reliance on crowdsourced peer advice. |
| **Metzger & Flanagin (2013)** | Cognitive Heuristics in Digital Environments | Conceptual framework; information credibility | Digital users rely on rapid mental shortcuts (social proof, bandwagon, familiarity heuristics) rather than systematic source verification. | Explains why students rely on Telegram peer consensus (likes, reactions, forward counts) without official confirmation. |
| **Awaki & Desta (2020)** | Systematic Literature Review | Systematic review of 30+ studies in Ethiopian higher education | Social media (Telegram, Facebook) enhances informal knowledge exchange and peer bonding, but suffers from rumors and lack of moderation. | Establishes the institutional reality of social media in Ethiopian universities; highlights absence of studies on academic advice trust. |
| **Cale & McNulty (2025)** | Source Credibility & Near-Peer Mentorship | Quantitative survey ($N=84$); medical students | Students rate institutionally-affiliated sources and near-peers as significantly more credible than generic online influencers. | Directly supports the hypothesis that near-peer seniors are trusted far more than anonymous internet posters. |
| **Dirin, Nieminen, & Alamäki (2021)** | Social Bonding & Decision-Making | Mixed-methods study in Finland higher education | Prospective and current students rely on informal peer social media channels when formal institutional guidance is opaque. | Demonstrates that institutional information deficits drive students to crowdsourced channels for study-path choices. |
| **Kassa (1999)** | Decision Support & Educational Placement | Institutional case study; Addis Ababa University | AAU uses centralized placement committees with strict intake caps for freshman department allocation, creating high competition. | Illustrates the high-stakes contextual pressures that motivate AAU freshmen to seek informal Telegram advice. |
